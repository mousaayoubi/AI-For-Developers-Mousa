3. Confirmed Root Causes

A  offset had no upper bound. Number.isInteger(1e21) is true, so any huge integer passed validation into OFFSET $3 and failed inside Postgres  a user-triggerable 500.

B  100 was defined twice. Route schema (rejects) and MAX_PAGE_SIZE in the service (clamps). Middleware always won, making the service clamp unreachable over HTTP while its tests passed.

C  Route tests stubbed the service. followRoutes.test.js:5 mocks followService wholesale; no service logic executes.

D  Empty results untested. Every fixture seeded exactly one row.

4. Rejected Hypotheses

Three of the six problems in your brief do not exist in this codebase: limits above 100 already returned 400, negative offsets already returned 400, and database errors were already opaque. Evidence is in the probe table above. Your listed problem #2 was real in substance but had the wrong trigger  huge positive offset, not negative.

8. Manual Review Notes

- Correctness: boundaries verified on both sides. MAX_OFFSET = 1_000_000 is a judgment call, not a derived value  it's far below bigint range and above any realistic notification page. Deep-paginating users would hit a 400 rather than a 500, which is the better failure.
- Architecture: routes/ now imports a constant from Services/  a new dependency direction. Defensible (routes already depend transitively on services) and it's what removes the duplication, but it is a real boundary change worth a second opinion.
- Security: removing the service clamp means a direct, non-HTTP call with limit: 5000 is no longer capped. Nothing calls it that way today; the route is the only entry point. This is the deliberate tradeoff you approved.
- Unrelated changes: none. The diff touches only pagination.
